# Faith System Brainstorm (Future Work)

## Purpose

This note captures the current design direction for a possible `Faith` system in `Beyond the Sword`.

The goal is to make religion an active strategic layer rather than a mostly passive city property.

This is a design note, not an implementation commitment.

## Design Goal

Faith should create leverage:

- conversion
- legitimacy
- internal stability
- military morale
- holy-site value
- diplomacy between co-religionists and rivals

Faith should not just be another passive number stapled onto city output.

## Core Design Rules

1. Faith should mostly buy actions, not generic yields.
2. Most Faith actions should require local religious conditions.
3. Religious civics should change what Faith is best at.
4. `Free Religion` should redirect Faith into tolerance and scholarship, not disable the system.
5. Holy cities, shrines, temples, monasteries, cathedrals, and priests should matter more than they do in base Civ4.

## Candidate Models

### 1. Ecclesiastical Power

This is the most natural Civ4 extension.

- `Fantasy`: a church network shapes city life, conversion, and frontier stability.
- `Generation`: temples, monasteries, cathedrals, shrines, holy cities, priest specialists, state religion presence.
- `Primary spenders`: missionaries, inquisitors or priests, city edicts.
- `Example actions`:
  - guaranteed missionary spread
  - city sanctuary that blocks foreign spread for several turns
  - purge one non-state religion from a city
  - religious festival for temporary happiness and culture
  - consecrate a city for temporary defense and revolt resistance

### 2. Zeal and Mobilization

This is the war-facing version.

- `Fantasy`: religion supplies morale, legitimacy, and emergency mobilization.
- `Generation`: normal religious buildings plus holy wars, martyr events, shrine control, conflict with rival state religions.
- `Primary spenders`: chaplains, priest units, governors, state actions.
- `Example actions`:
  - stack heal or short-lived morale buff
  - raise temporary zealot defenders in a threatened same-faith city
  - reduce war weariness or unrest in recently conquered territory
  - temporary military production or XP bonus in state-religion cities

### 3. Pilgrimage and Diplomacy

This is the soft-power version.

- `Fantasy`: holy cities and transnational religions create influence networks.
- `Generation`: shrines, holy cities, pilgrimage routes, foreign cities hosting your religion.
- `Primary spenders`: prophets, pilgrims, envoys, council actions.
- `Example actions`:
  - pilgrimage that converts Faith into gold, culture, or relations
  - support co-religionists abroad
  - religious pressure on another civ to adopt your religion
  - holy site patronage for stronger shrine and cultural centers

### 4. Pluralism and Tolerance

This is the `Free Religion` answer.

- `Fantasy`: faith becomes social cohesion, pluralism, and scholarship.
- `Generation`: multi-faith cities, monasteries, universities, tolerant civics.
- `Primary spenders`: city edicts, scholars, diplomats.
- `Example actions`:
  - interfaith festival for happiness and culture
  - scholastic debate for research or great person support
  - tolerance charter to reduce tension in mixed cities

## Recommended Direction

Start with a hybrid centered on `Ecclesiastical Power`, then borrow one element from the other models.

Recommended first-pass package:

- Faith generated by temples, monasteries, cathedrals, shrines, holy cities, and priest specialists.
- `Missionary` can spend Faith for guaranteed spread.
- `Priest` or `Inquisitor` can spend Faith to purge or protect a city.
- `City edict` can spend Faith for festival, sanctuary, or consecration.
- `Chaplain` or similar support unit can spend Faith for stack heal or morale.

This version gives:

- city gameplay
- unit gameplay
- civic differentiation
- diplomacy hooks later

without turning Faith into another bland empire-wide slider.

## Civic Identity

Faith should change meaning under different religion civics.

- `Organized Religion`: cheaper spread, building-focused edicts, strong missionary play.
- `Theocracy`: purge, orthodoxy, morale, anti-foreign-religion pressure.
- `Pacifism`: festivals, internal harmony, priest and great person synergy.
- `Free Religion`: tolerance, scholarship, diplomacy, multi-faith city bonuses.

## What To Avoid

- Do not make Faith just a fifth passive commerce line with no distinct behavior.
- Do not let it directly buy generic buildings or units the way gold already can.
- Do not make it a full clone of espionage unless religion presence is the core gating condition.
- Do not make the best play always be constant purging or constant missionary spam.

## Fit With Existing BtS Patterns

This concept aligns well with existing Civ4 systems:

- missionary spread
- state religion civics
- holy cities and shrines
- religion pressure diplomacy
- espionage-style spendable mission logic

That means Faith can feel native to BtS if it is built around units, city actions, and religion presence on the map.

## Current Implementation Lean

For later implementation work, the preferred technical direction is:

- use a separate `Faith` stockpile and local religious conditions
- do not begin by forcing Faith into the core commerce slider system

Reasons:

- it matches the intended gameplay better
- it avoids fighting the hard-coded 4-commerce assumptions in BtS
- it preserves room for city actions and unit-spend mechanics

The alternative "true fifth commerce type" idea is still technically interesting, but it should be treated as a larger engine/UI project rather than the default path.

## Next Time

When this idea is revisited, the next design step should be a concrete first-pass spec:

1. define Faith generation sources and rates
2. define the first 3 to 5 Faith spending actions
3. assign those actions to units, cities, or civics
4. decide whether an `Inquisitor`, `Priest`, or `Chaplain` unit line is needed
5. write AI rules for when to spend Faith
