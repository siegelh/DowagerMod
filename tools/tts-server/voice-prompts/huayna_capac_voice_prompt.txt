Male, middle-aged, Andean imperial. The Sapa Inca — Sole Sovereign, Son of the Sun — speaks with the formal bearing of someone who administers four quarters of the world. Peruvian-Andean accent: slightly elevated vowels, the highland tonality of the Cusco region, the measured cadence of Quechua ceremonial speech beneath a Spanish acoustic approximation. Dignified, solemn, slightly melancholy — an emperor at the height of an empire that is about to end in ways he does not yet know. Not cruel or bombastic; imperial in the way that a road network stretching 4,000km is imperial — practical, total, and deeply proud. Pauses that carry the silence of high altitude. Think: a man whose roads were better than Rome's, whose administrative system fed millions, whose realm stretched from equatorial forest to Antarctic steppe.
