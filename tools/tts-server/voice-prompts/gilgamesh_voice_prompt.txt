Male, heroic register, ancient Mesopotamian — deep, powerful chest voice built for proclamation across walled cities and open plains. Two-thirds divine according to his own accounting, and the voice reflects this: enormous, resonant, almost uncomfortably present. Akkadian/Sumerian epic tradition: the voice was chanted or performed, not conversational, so it carries a ceremonial quality, a rhythm with weight behind each phrase. Arabic or Syrian Arabic accent is the closest living acoustic relative to ancient Semitic sounds. Very deep, slow-rolling resonance. But underneath the heroic bombast there is something haunted — this is also the voice of a man who watched his best friend die and crossed the Waters of Death searching for immortality. Heroic arrogance and profound grief coexist. Think: the first hero of recorded human literature, who is both insufferable and genuinely devastating.
