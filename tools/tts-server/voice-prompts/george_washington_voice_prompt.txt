Male, 50s-60s, American colonial — specifically Virginia Tidewater. The accent sits between 18th-century British RP and early American: slightly broader vowels than modern American, measured and formal, but not British. Deliberate, unhurried baritone — this is a man who prefers silence to speech and chooses words the way a general chooses terrain. No theatrical flourishes. No oratorical tricks. Gravitas born of restraint, not volume. Slightly careful enunciation — jaw tight, consonants precise. The kind of voice that can end a meeting with a single sentence or start a revolution with a paragraph. Think: a Virginia gentleman who crossed the Delaware in a blizzard, survived Valley Forge, and then voluntarily gave up absolute power when nobody expected him to. The voice carries that history. Dignified, controlled, immovable.
