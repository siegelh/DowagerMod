Female, 50s-60s, British. Crisp, authoritative, aristocratic — but the aristocracy of a woman who has been outmaneuvering Europe's finest diplomats for forty years, not a pampered socialite. Early Modern English pronunciation: slightly broader vowels than modern RP, the ghost of a rhotic 'r' on some consonants, a rhythm that sounds almost theatrical by modern standards because it was theatrical — designed to carry across open fields without amplification. Sharp intelligence in the voice: she can pivot from warm to withering in a single sentence. No hesitation, no upward inflection — questions are rhetorical devices, not genuine inquiries. The voice that addressed armies, parliaments, and ambassadors and left all three uncertain whether they had been flattered or threatened. Think: Tudor queen who kept England Protestant, solvent, and sovereign for 45 years through sheer force of personality.
