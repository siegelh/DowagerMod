Male, 60s-70s. Upper-class British English of the Edwardian era — rounded vowels, aristocratic precision, the grander diction of early 20th-century public life. Historically documented lateral lisp: a slight sibilant distortion on the "s" sound that Churchill weaponized into a rhetorical quirk rather than hiding it. Deliberate, rolling cadence with strategic pauses deployed as weapons. Resonant working baritone, not especially deep but carrying. Builds through a sentence toward emphasis — each clause a step toward a devastating final word. Equally at home in cigar-room sarcasm and the sweeping call-to-arms. A man who stood between civilization and barbarism and found it exhilarating.
