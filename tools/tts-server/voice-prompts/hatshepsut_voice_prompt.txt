Female, mature 30s-40s, Egyptian. A woman's voice carrying a king's authority — and aware that these are supposed to be the same thing. Ancient Egyptian court formality: measured, regal, precise. Arabic-Egyptian phonology as the closest acoustic relative to ancient Egyptian — warm, rounded consonants, the formal register of a royal court. She speaks as king, not as queen — no softness deployed as performance, no apology for authority. The voice is deliberate and architectural: she built the most beautiful mortuary temple in Egypt and her sentences have the same considered structure. Not aggressive — permanent. Her stepson spent thirty years destroying her monuments; she had already made enough of them that it didn't matter. Think: a woman who wore the double crown and false beard of pharaoh and made it look inevitable.
